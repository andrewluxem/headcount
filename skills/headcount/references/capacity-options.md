# Capacity Options

Use this reference to match a capacity mechanism to the type and duration of demand.

## Demand classes

### Fixed or core demand

Work that persists across credible scenarios and is necessary for a durable outcome or guardrail. It may support fixed capacity when the zero-based role test and total-cost review are satisfied.

### Variable or scalable demand

Work that changes with season, volume, projects, or uncertainty. It may favor scope triggers, flexible processes, tools, shared capacity, or time-bound capacity, subject to authorized review.

### Unclear demand

Work with missing volume, duration, effort, or consequence evidence. Use a bounded measurement or pilot before making a hard-to-reverse commitment when feasible.

## Comparable units

Choose a unit that connects demand to capacity, such as reviewed cases per week, completed analyses per month, or supported operating hours. Use sustainable capacity, not a theoretical maximum. Keep these aligned:

- same work definition;
- same quality standard;
- same period;
- same included activities;
- same guardrails.

If units differ, do not calculate the gap. State what must be converted or measured and who owns it.

## Option ladder

Consider each feasible category:

1. Keep the current plan and gather evidence.
2. Stop or defer lower-priority work.
3. Simplify, standardize, or remove rework.
4. Use an approved tool or automation with human controls.
5. Share or reallocate capacity through an authorized people process.
6. Use time-bound external or temporary capacity through authorized review.
7. Add fixed headcount through authorized finance and people review.

The order supports complete comparison. It does not presume that a lower-numbered option is preferable.

## Common comparison fields

For every viable option, assess:

- strategic outcome coverage;
- fit for fixed, variable, or unclear demand;
- time to useful capacity;
- supplied total-cost inputs;
- reversibility and exit conditions;
- capability and quality coverage;
- privacy, safety, continuity, and security effects;
- employment, procurement, legal, and people-process review;
- dependency and management load.

Unknown fields stay unknown. Do not assign a favorable rank merely because an option lacks cost or risk data.

## Cost construction

Possible inputs include base compensation or service fees, employer or contract costs, recruiting or procurement, tools and equipment, setup and transition, training, management, support, and exit costs. Include only supplied reviewable inputs and place them on the same period.

When the necessary figures are supplied:

`total cost = sum of compatible included cost inputs`

`cost percentage of revenue = total cost / revenue for the same period * 100`

The calculation is not a benchmark, budget approval, return forecast, or financial recommendation. Record exclusions and require human finance validation.

## Scenarios and triggers

Use low, expected, and high scenarios only when supported by supplied assumptions. Do not assign probabilities unless the user supplies an approved method and inputs. Each scenario needs:

- demand assumption;
- capacity choice;
- cost basis;
- trigger to change course;
- human owner;
- review date.

## Reversibility

Compare the time, cost, people impact, continuity risk, and approval required to increase, reduce, or exit an option. A flexible option is not automatically safe, cheap, or responsible. A fixed option is not automatically wasteful. The evidence and decision horizon determine the fit.
