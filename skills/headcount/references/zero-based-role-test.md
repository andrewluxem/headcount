# Zero-Based Role Test

Use this test to justify a role from future work and outcomes rather than history.

## 1. Strategic alignment

- Which approved strategic outcome requires this capacity?
- Who is the customer or beneficiary?
- What observable consequence occurs if the work is not done?
- What supplied evidence connects the work to the outcome?

A role that supports no named outcome is not ready for approval.

## 2. Recurring outcome ownership

Define the durable result the role would own, not a list of disconnected tasks. Identify work that recurs across the planning horizon and work that exists only for a temporary peak, transition, or project.

Test: If the temporary work disappeared, would a coherent full role still remain?

## 3. Capability need

Name the capability, required level, decision scope, and frequency of use. Distinguish:

- capability missing from the current system;
- capability present but fully committed;
- capability available through a stable interface;
- capability needed only intermittently;
- capability gap caused by unclear process or priorities.

Do not assume a title is the only way to supply a capability.

## 4. Sustainable demand

Use supplied volume, effort, frequency, duration, and service expectations in comparable units. Separate:

- fixed or core demand that persists across scenarios;
- variable or scalable demand that changes with volume, season, or projects;
- unknown demand that needs a bounded test.

Busy periods, backlogs, and complaints may be signals, but they do not establish sustainable demand without context.

## 5. Current-capacity test

Show current sustainable capacity, existing commitments, wait, rework, and guardrail effects. Avoid theoretical maximum capacity. A valid gap uses the same unit and period on both sides.

## 6. Alternatives before a role

Ask what could be stopped, deferred, simplified, standardized, supported by an approved tool, served through an interface, shared, or covered with time-bound capacity. Record why each viable option does or does not address the outcome and risk.

The purpose is not to prevent hiring. It is to show why the recommended capacity mechanism fits the demand better than feasible alternatives.

## 7. Role boundary and measures

Define:

- recurring outcome and decisions owned;
- core capabilities;
- work explicitly outside the role;
- interfaces with adjacent roles or teams;
- supplied baseline and target, or an evidence gap;
- guardrail measures;
- review owner and date.

## Invalid shortcuts

Do not use these as the sole justification:

- the role existed before;
- another team or peer has the title;
- the team is busy without comparable demand and capacity evidence;
- a manager prefers a larger organization;
- an unsupported industry benchmark;
- a claim that the role will pay for itself without reviewable inputs.

## Approval boundary

The test produces a role case for authorized human review. It does not approve budget, open a role, set compensation, change employment, or determine compliance.
